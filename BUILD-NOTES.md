# Build notes — Worst Superpower

The bounty asks for one line, at most 140 characters, inventing the most
useless superpower imaginable. A deadpan judge compares entries two at a
time and weighs uselessness first, then how funny the power is to picture
in use, then how unexpected it is, then how tight and specific the wording
is, and finally how clear it is on a single reading.

The cliché radar ruled out the lane nine out of ten entrants will take:
flying a few inches off the ground, turning invisible while nobody watches,
talking to animals that ignore you, super strength for opening jars, and
time travel a few seconds into the past. The brief itself names slow flight
and unwatched invisibility as familiar gags, so both were avoided.

Three contrasting concepts were scored on hook, originality, sponsor fit,
and clarity, each out of ten. The deadpan paradox concept, sensing a
printer jam only after it has already jammed, scored 35. The cultural hook
concept, ending meetings on time but only meetings the hero is not invited
to, scored 33. The absurdist image concept, talking to fish but only the
ones in the supermarket freezer, also scored 35, and won the tie on the
judge's own criteria in order: its uselessness is total, because dead fish
have nothing to say; its picture in use, a hero earnestly chatting with
frozen cod in a supermarket aisle, is the funniest of the three; and
nobody has heard that exact power before. The power harms nobody, so it is
not a curse, and it helps nobody, so it is not quietly useful.

The winning line is 65 characters, one line, no trailing newline. The
program writes it to the output file and mirrors the gate in code: the text
must be between 1 and 140 characters, a single line, and not only white
space. A fresh run was checked to produce byte-identical output, and the
kit check was run from the kit directory.
